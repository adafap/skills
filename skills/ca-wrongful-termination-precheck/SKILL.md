# California Wrongful Termination Pre-check

Create a careful issue-spotting summary, not a legal conclusion. Distinguish lawful at-will termination from an allegedly unlawful reason for termination. Treat every filing deadline as urgent and source-dependent.

Read [sources.md](references/sources.md) before stating California law, protected categories, agency routes, or deadlines. Recheck the linked primary sources when the answer will be used after the reference retrieval date.

## Intake

Collect only facts needed for issue spotting. Ask one compact follow-up when required facts are missing.

Require:

- whether the work was performed in California;
- termination, notice, or forced-resignation date;
- employer-stated reason and what happened immediately before it;
- worker status, employer type, and any written contract, union agreement, or fixed term;
- protected activity or status potentially connected to the decision;
- documents and witnesses already available;
- the user's immediate goal.

Ask for employer size only when a protection may depend on it. Accept `unknown`. Never request a Social Security number, full birth date, home address, medical records, account credentials, or unredacted confidential documents.

## Workflow

1. Restate the material facts and label every unknown. Keep allegations attributed to the user.
2. Explain the at-will baseline without implying that at-will employment permits an unlawful reason.
3. Check these issue families separately:
   - discrimination or harassment tied to a protected characteristic;
   - retaliation for asserting civil-rights, leave, accommodation, wage, safety, or other workplace rights;
   - whistleblowing or refusal to participate in suspected unlawful conduct;
   - protected leave, disability, pregnancy, or accommodation activity;
   - contract, fixed-term, union, public-sector, or employer-policy restrictions;
   - constructive discharge, mass layoff, final-pay, or other adjacent issues that require separate review.
4. For each possible issue, connect only the supplied facts to the source-grounded rule. State missing facts and competing explanations.
5. Build a deadline-urgency map by agency or claim family. Say `verify immediately` when the route or deadline depends on facts. Never merge CRD, DLSE, EEOC, OSHA, union, contract, or court deadlines into one generic period.
6. Produce an evidence-preservation checklist. Prefer lawful copies already accessible to the worker; warn against deleting, altering, secretly accessing, or taking privileged, proprietary, or third-party personal information.
7. Recommend the smallest safe next step, including prompt consultation with a California employment lawyer or the relevant agency when a deadline or serious issue may exist.

## Output contract

Return these sections:

1. `Scope and limits`
2. `Facts provided`
3. `Important unknowns`
4. `Issue pre-check` as a table with issue, supporting facts, missing or adverse facts, primary source, and status (`signal`, `unclear`, or `not indicated`)
5. `Deadline urgency` with separate routes and a current-source verification note
6. `Evidence to preserve`
7. `Lawyer-ready summary` in neutral chronological prose
8. `Next questions and actions`

Use calibrated language such as `may indicate`, `needs review`, and `not enough information`. Never say that a termination was definitely wrongful, assign a probability of winning, estimate damages or settlement value, or promise an agency or court outcome.

## Stop conditions

- If the work was not performed in California, stop the California analysis and ask for the governing jurisdiction.
- If the termination or notice date is missing, provide only a provisional issue map and ask for the date before discussing deadlines.
- If a deadline may be imminent, lead with urgency and primary agency links.
- If the user asks to file, contact an employer, send a demand, or communicate with an agency, explain the separate authorization and identity requirements; do not perform the external action under this Skill.
- If current primary sources are unavailable, label every legal rule and deadline unverified and recommend immediate professional or agency confirmation.
